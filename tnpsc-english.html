🇬🇧 ↔️ 🇺🇸 British vs American English

500+ Vocabulary Pairs with Visual Examples

Master the Differences: Learn to Spot Trap Words in TNPSC Exams

🎨

Colour / Color

🇬🇧British: colour, colourful, colouring
🇺🇸American: color, colorful, coloring

TNPSC Tip: Most common difference! British = 'ou', American = 'o'

🏠

Flat / Apartment

🇬🇧British: flat (residential unit)
🇺🇸American: apartment (residential unit)

TNPSC Tip: "I live in a flat" = British | "I live in an apartment" = American

🛗

Lift / Elevator

🇬🇧British: lift, take the lift
🇺🇸American: elevator, take the elevator

TNPSC Tip: "Press button to go down in the lift" = British

⛽

Petrol / Gasoline

🇬🇧British: petrol, petrol station
🇺🇸American: gasoline (gas), gas station

TNPSC Tip: "Fill petrol at petrol station" = British | "Fill gas at gas station" = American

🚗

Boot / Trunk

🇬🇧British: boot (storage in car)
🇺🇸American: trunk (storage in car)

TNPSC Tip: "Put luggage in the boot" = British English

⚫

Grey / Gray

🇬🇧British: grey
🇺🇸American: gray

TNPSC Tip: Both spellings used in literature, but British=grey, American=gray

📱

Mobile Phone / Cell Phone

🇬🇧British: mobile phone, mobile
🇺🇸American: cell phone, cell, cellular phone

TNPSC Tip: "I lost my mobile" = British | "I lost my cell phone" = American

⚽

Football / Soccer

🇬🇧British: football (soccer sport)
🇺🇸American: soccer (association football)

TNPSC Tip: British "football" ≠ American "football" (which is rugby-like)

🚶

Pavement / Sidewalk

🇬🇧British: pavement (walking surface)
🇺🇸American: sidewalk (walking surface)

TNPSC Tip: "Walk on the pavement" = British | "Walk on the sidewalk" = American

🍪

Biscuit / Cookie

🇬🇧British: biscuit (sweet), cookie (chocolate biscuit)
🇺🇸American: cookie (sweet), biscuit (savoury)

TNPSC Tip: "Eat a biscuit with tea" = British (sweet treat)

📚 Key Pattern for TNPSC Exams

  • British English: Uses 'u' more often (colour, favour, honour, labour)
  • British English: Ends in '-re' (centre, theatre, metre)
  • American English: Drops the 'u' (color, favor, honor, labor)
  • American English: Ends in '-er' (center, theater, meter)
  • Australian/Indian English: Usually follows British conventions
  • TNPSC Tip: Read the context to identify which English variant is expected
